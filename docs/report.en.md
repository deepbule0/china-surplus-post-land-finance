# China’s Trade Surplus and the Transition Beyond Land Finance

[中文](report.zh-CN.md) · [Project overview](../README.md)

*13 October 2026. Perspective: China’s long-term economic interests. Recommendations are analytical judgments; announced policies are identified separately.*

## 1. Core conclusion

China’s large external surplus, weak household demand, and declining reliance on land-based local-government finance are connected. A durable adjustment should combine **higher household purchasing power with stable funding for public services**.

The aim should be a sustainable external balance, strong manufacturing, and higher household welfare. Fiscal reform and immediate household support can proceed together; assistance need not wait for a complete redesign of taxation.

## 2. What the numbers mean

| Indicator | Figure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customs goods-trade surplus, 2025 | About **US$1.189 trillion** [1] | An annual flow, not government profit or an equivalent increase in FX reserves. |
| Current-account surplus, 2025 | **US$735 billion** [2] | Includes services and income; customs and balance-of-payments goods measures also differ. |
| Local land-use-right transfer revenue, 2021 | **RMB8.705 trillion** [3] | Gross government-managed funds receipts. |
| Same revenue, 2025 | **RMB4.152 trillion** [4] | Approximately **52.3% below 2021**, calculated from final accounts. |
| Local general-budget own-source revenue, 2025 | **RMB12.208 trillion** [5] | Excludes central transfers. |
| Local general-budget expenditure, 2025 | **RMB24.436 trillion**, about **85%** of national spending [5] | Local governments deliver much of public spending. |
| Central general-budget transfers to local governments, 2025 | **RMB10.193 trillion** [5] | Transfers already fund much of local expenditure. |

Land revenue belongs to the **government-managed funds budget**, separate from the general public budget. Receipts also finance land acquisition, compensation, and development. A fall in gross receipts is therefore not an equal loss of freely spendable revenue and cannot simply be added to the general-budget gap.

The relevant accounting identity is:

$$\text{Current-account balance} = \text{National saving} - \text{Domestic investment}.$$

It describes the surplus’s domestic counterpart, not its causes by itself. Foreign claims are held by firms, banks, investors, and monetary authorities; they are not all dollars or official reserves. Currency losses depend on denomination and hedging, while total returns also depend on income, asset prices, and purchasing power.

A positive surplus can support future imports and an ageing population. An exceptionally large one can increase dependence on foreign demand and exposure to trade disputes. Portfolio diversification addresses asset risk; it does not repair weak domestic demand.

## 3. Why land finance matters for the surplus

Our interpretation is that declining land revenue can reinforce the imbalance through two channels:

- **Demand:** spending restraint, contractor arrears, and uncertainty about public services can weaken incomes and increase precautionary saving.
- **Supply:** localities seeking tax revenue and jobs may continue competing for factories and industrial projects, including projects with weak commercial returns.

Weak consumption alongside expanding production can increase reliance on exports. This is a plausible mechanism, not a quantified explanation of the entire surplus. Productivity, foreign demand, supply chains, and exchange rates also matter; fiscal pressure can also constrain industrial investment.

The underlying fiscal challenge is to align spending obligations with stable funding and improve incentives to support residents and services. Transfers are a normal part of a fiscal system, so the difference between local own-source revenue and expenditure is not, by itself, evidence of insolvency.

## 4. Historical lessons

| Experience | Lesson |
| --- | --- |
| China before the global financial crisis | High investment can coexist with still higher saving and a large surplus. More investment alone does not guarantee rebalancing. [6] |
| China after the crisis | Higher domestic investment and imports helped reduce the surplus, alongside weaker foreign demand and other changes. Investment quality and financing matter. [7] |
| Japan after the 1985 Plaza Accord | Currency appreciation, prolonged easing, lending incentives, and asset expectations interacted. The bubble cannot be attributed to appreciation alone. [8] |

The implication is to avoid relying exclusively on another property-and-infrastructure surge or an abrupt currency adjustment.

## 5. A practical policy package

1. **Protect services and clear arrears.** Make transfers predictable, prioritize basic services, and settle verified overdue payments. Pair debt restructuring with controls on new off-budget borrowing.

2. **Raise household income and security.** Strengthen basic pensions, healthcare, unemployment protection, childcare, and migrant workers’ access to services. Credible funding can reduce the need to save against future emergencies.

3. **Rebuild local revenue incentives.** Implement suitable consumption-tax reforms and review shared-tax allocation and needs-based transfers. China’s consumption tax is a selective excise, not a broad retail sales tax. Moving collection downstream does not automatically allocate revenue to the place of consumption.

4. **Match fiscal capacity with responsibilities.** Consider greater central or provincial funding for national minimum service standards, with clear accountability and effective local delivery.

5. **Sequence new taxes carefully.** Recurrent property taxation could provide a stable local base over time. During a housing adjustment, valuation, exemptions, household cash flow, and deferrals require care. Avoid replacing land receipts primarily through broad taxes or fees on ordinary households. No rollout date is predicted here.

6. **Adjust the external balance gradually.** Expand useful imports and domestic services, retain exchange-rate flexibility, and assess overseas investments by returns, risk, and liquidity. Outbound investment changes the financing or investment counterpart of a surplus; it does **not directly reduce the current account**. RMB settlement can change currency exposure without removing the saving–investment imbalance.

Household spending would not reduce the surplus one-for-one: domestic output, imports, investment, and saving would all respond. The appropriate surplus level depends on these outcomes; a fixed dollar target is not justified here.

## 6. Announced direction versus proposals

| Measure | Status |
| --- | --- |
| More local tax sources, improved transfers, downstream consumption-tax collection with gradual local allocation, and greater central spending responsibility | Directions in the **2024 Third Plenum decision**; announcement does not establish completed implementation. [9] |
| Local hidden-debt replacement framework | **RMB6 trillion** of additional local debt limits plus **RMB4 trillion** through five years of special-bond arrangements. Multi-year debt resolution, not RMB10 trillion of new household stimulus. [10] |
| Central assumption of substantial local debt | A possible further proposal. Existing swaps generally issue formal **local-government bonds**, rather than automatically making the central government liable. [10] |
| Destination-based VAT sharing and carefully phased recurrent property taxation | Analytical proposals here, not claims of completed nationwide reforms. |

Debt swaps can ease repayment and interest burdens. They cannot permanently fix a recurring mismatch between spending obligations and sustainable revenue.

## 7. What to watch

Look for implemented tax-allocation rules, predictable transfers, dependable welfare funding, arrears clearance, and limits on renewed hidden debt. Judge progress through real household income, consumption, service reliability, and the current account—not just a headline deficit or debt quota.

Late-2026 policy meetings and the 2027 NPC budget process are useful checkpoints. This report asserts neither exact future meeting dates nor a guaranteed reform package.
